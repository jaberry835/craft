# Scope Change Impact Review

## Change Request
The curriculum must now include the following additional content domains:
- Energy Security
- Climate Policy
- Critical Infrastructure

## Review Summary
The current 6-month curriculum is built around three original focus areas:
- Technology Policy
- Economics
- Public Health

The new requirement expands the domain scope substantially. This is not a minor content insertion. It affects curriculum framing, monthly sequencing, learning objectives, source-text selection, practical exercises, and assessment coverage.

## Current State Review
The following control documents still reflect the earlier scope and will require revision:
- Program profile and focus areas in the root README
- Curriculum map and monthly thematic progression
- Program syllabus course arc
- Program-level ILR-3 learning objectives
- Unit plans, lesson plans, assessments, and language resources that currently center only the original domains

## Impact Analysis

### 1. Program Positioning
Impact: High

The curriculum is currently described as a three-domain program. Adding three new domains changes the program identity from a narrower policy-economics-health arc to a broader strategic policy and infrastructure curriculum.

Recommended change:
- Revise the program profile to name all six domains explicitly.
- Decide whether the curriculum remains a 6-month program with redistributed emphasis, or whether duration must expand.

### 2. Scope and Sequence
Impact: High

The existing six-month arc has no dedicated month for Energy Security, Climate Policy, or Critical Infrastructure.

Recommended change:
- Rebuild the curriculum map so the new domains are either assigned dedicated months or integrated into cross-sector units.
- Rework Month 5 and Month 6 to carry more deliberate multi-domain synthesis if no extension is approved.

### 3. Learning Objectives
Impact: High

Current ILR-3 objectives cover technology regulation, economic trends, and public health policy only.

Recommended change:
- Add program-level objectives for discussing energy security, climate policy, and critical infrastructure in Arabic at ILR-3.
- Update can-do statements to reflect comparative analysis across all six domains.

### 4. Unit and Lesson Design
Impact: High

The existing unit structure supports governance, economics, public health, and cross-sector synthesis, but it does not yet allocate instructional time for the new domains.

Recommended change:
- Add or revise unit plans to incorporate energy supply security, infrastructure resilience, climate governance, and interdependence across sectors.
- Add new lesson-plan sequences or refactor existing Month 5 cross-sector materials.

### 5. Student Materials and Resources
Impact: Medium to High

Current vocabulary lists, reading packets, and source-text plans do not cover the new subject-matter language.

Recommended change:
- Build new terminology banks for energy security, climate policy, and critical infrastructure.
- Add Arabic readings and discussion packets for each new domain.
- Add authentic source-text sets tied to policy, market, and security perspectives.

### 6. Practical Exercises
Impact: Medium to High

Existing exercises are still usable as models, but the scenario inventory is too narrow.

Recommended change:
- Add scenario-based speaking exercises on grid resilience, energy disruption, climate negotiations, and infrastructure vulnerability.
- Add cross-domain simulations linking AI systems, infrastructure risk, public health continuity, and economic stability.

### 7. Assessments
Impact: Medium to High

Current assessment artifacts can still be reused structurally, but they do not yet sample the new content domains.

Recommended change:
- Update formative and integrated assessments to include prompts and listening passages from the new domains.
- Revise rubrics only if the program wants explicit domain-breadth criteria; otherwise the current analytic rubric can remain largely stable.

## Recommended Change Path

### Option A: Keep 6 Months, Rebalance Content
Best when schedule cannot change.

Recommended approach:
- Month 1: Technology Policy and AI Regulation Foundations
- Month 2: Governance, Regulation, and Critical Infrastructure
- Month 3: Economics, Energy Security, and Strategic Markets
- Month 4: Public Health and Climate Risk Policy
- Month 5: Cross-Sector Resilience and Infrastructure Stress Scenarios
- Month 6: Capstone Performance and Integrated Simulation

Tradeoff:
Coverage per domain becomes shallower, and instructors will need tighter prioritization.

### Option B: Expand Beyond 6 Months
Best when full domain depth is required.

Recommended approach:
- Preserve the current three-domain structure
- Add dedicated units for Energy Security, Climate Policy, and Critical Infrastructure
- Extend the program to allow domain depth rather than compression

Tradeoff:
Requires schedule, staffing, and material-production changes.

### Option C: Keep Core Arc, Add Supplementary Modules
Best when the new domains are important but secondary.

Recommended approach:
- Preserve the six core months
- Add modular inserts, briefing labs, and optional readings on the three new domains
- Use Month 5 and Month 6 for integrated comparative tasks

Tradeoff:
New domains may appear underweighted relative to the requirement wording.

## Decision Points
The following decisions should be made before revising downstream files:

### Decision Point 1: Program Duration
Prompt:
Should the curriculum remain 6 months, or should duration expand to preserve depth across six domains?

### Decision Point 2: Domain Weighting
Prompt:
Are Energy Security, Climate Policy, and Critical Infrastructure equal-priority focus areas, or supporting domains within the broader curriculum?

### Decision Point 3: Structural Model
Prompt:
Should the new domains receive dedicated units, or should they be embedded within economics, governance, and cross-sector analysis?

### Decision Point 4: Assessment Coverage
Prompt:
Must all formal assessments sample all six domains, or is selective domain coverage acceptable across the program?

### Decision Point 5: Resource Development Scope
Prompt:
Should the project generate full new vocabulary, reading, exercise, and assessment sets for all three new domains now, or phase them in over time?

## Recommendation
The most defensible path is Option A only if the user accepts broader but shallower coverage. If the new domains are mandatory and equal in importance to the existing three, Option B is academically stronger and operationally cleaner.
